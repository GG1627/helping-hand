# P2: Beta Presentation Guidelines

## Overview

The **Beta Presentation** is an **8–10 minute presentation** that provides an update on the project since the prototype presentation and presents an updated iteration of the project plan.

All team members should participate.

Following the presentation, there will be approximately **2–3 minutes for questions** from instructors and students.

The presentation should focus on:

- The **vertical slice of features** included in the alpha build.
- The **full set of features** included in the beta build.
- The project's current state of development.
- The updated plan for completing and polishing the final deliverables.

---

## Target Audience

The presentation should be designed for a **technical audience**, but the audience should **not be assumed to be experts in the project's specific subfield**.

Technical concepts should therefore be explained clearly enough that someone with a general technical background can understand the project.

---

## Context

The presentation must include a clear and concise explanation of the problem the project is addressing.

This section should answer:

- What need or problem exists?
- Why is the problem important?
- What is the project?
- How does the project address or solve the identified problem?

The project description should be brief but informative.

---

## Delivery

The presentation should be delivered in a **professional, clear, and straightforward manner**.

### Presentation Requirements

- All props, devices, software, and other presentation materials must be prepared in advance.
- Speakers should dress professionally in **business casual or formal attire** appropriate for a technical presentation.
- The presentation should have a clear and logical flow.
- Speakers should maintain appropriate pacing and avoid unnecessary or overly lengthy explanations.
- Speech should be clear and intelligible.
- Intonation and delivery should help maintain audience engagement.
- Visual and auditory elements should complement one another.
- Slides, demonstrations, and spoken explanations should be synchronized.

Evaluation will consider:

- Intonation
- Concision
- Intelligibility
- Presentation flow
- Pacing
- Media integration

---

## Project State

The presentation should explain all significant elements of the project's **current development state**.

The team should provide updates on features that are:

- **Completed**
- **In Progress**
- **Planned**

These updates should reflect any changes that have occurred since the previous presentation.

The presentation should make it clear how the project has progressed from the prototype and alpha stages into the beta build.

---

## Demonstration

A **live demonstration** of the project's current state and usability must be included.

The demonstration should clearly show:

- Major project features.
- The current functionality of those features.
- The usability of the project.
- The current state of development.

### Live Demo Requirement

The demonstration must be a **live user-case demonstration**.

Someone on the team should actively use the project's artifact(s) during the presentation in their current or very recent state.

The demonstration should **not be prerecorded**.

### Research Project Exception

For research projects that primarily produce knowledge rather than an artifact, the team may discuss the current state of the research instead of performing a traditional live demonstration.

This should be coordinated with the project's faculty advisor and stakeholder.

The research discussion should include, at minimum:

- Background and prior work in the field.
- Methods and procedures used in the research.
- Current findings.
- Implications of the work.

---

## Deliverable Plan

The team must present an **updated deliverable plan** based on current progress and project goals.

The plan should include:

- A timeline for completing each remaining section of the application or project.
- A timeline for polishing completed features.
- Specific tasks that still need to be completed.
- Dates associated with those tasks.
- A plan of action explaining how the tasks will be accomplished.

Tasks do **not** need to be assigned to specific individuals, although team member assignments may be included when useful for context.

The deliverable plan should demonstrate that the team has a realistic strategy for moving from the beta build to the final release.

---

## Overall Impression

The presentation will also be evaluated based on the overall quality and professionalism of the project and presentation.

Software and other technical artifacts have subjective qualities that may not fit directly into the other grading categories.

Evaluators may use their **industry experience and professional judgment** when assessing these qualities.

This category may consider aspects such as:

- Overall polish.
- Professional appearance.
- Usability.
- Technical maturity.
- Cohesiveness of the project.
- Quality of the presentation.
- Adherence to project requirements from the design prototype specification.

---

## Submission

Teams will:

1. Deliver the presentation **live**.
2. Submit a copy of all presentation materials, including **slides and/or props**, through **Canvas**.

---

## Quick Presentation Checklist

Before presenting, verify that the presentation includes:

- [ ] Clear explanation of the problem or need.
- [ ] Brief description of the project and proposed solution.
- [ ] Update on progress since the prototype presentation.
- [ ] Alpha-build vertical slice discussion.
- [ ] Beta-build feature discussion.
- [ ] Completed, in-progress, and planned feature status.
- [ ] Live user-case demonstration.
- [ ] Updated deliverable plan.
- [ ] Specific remaining tasks and dates.
- [ ] Plan of action for completing remaining work.
- [ ] Professional presentation flow and delivery.
- [ ] All props, hardware, software, and demo materials prepared in advance.
- [ ] All team members participate.
- [ ] Presentation fits within the **8–10 minute** time limit.
- [ ] Slides and other required presentation materials are ready for Canvas submission.
